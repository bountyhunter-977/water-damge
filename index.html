WHY ACT QUICKLY?

Small leaks can create larger problems

Water may travel under flooring, behind walls, and into cabinets. Prompt attention can help limit additional damage and make it easier to document the affected property.

  • Reduce continued water exposure.
  • Identify areas that need drying.
  • Protect belongings where possible.
  • Keep a clear record for your insurer.

Common signs of hidden water

  • Soft or buckled flooring
  • Discolored drywall or ceilings
  • Musty odors
  • Swollen cabinets or trim
  • Peeling paint or bubbling surfaces

PREVENT FUTURE WATER DAMAGE

Simple habits that can protect your home

Regular attention to plumbing fixtures and water-use patterns may help you identify problems before they become major leaks.

  • Know where your main water shutoff valve is located.
  • Inspect exposed supply lines and connections.
  • Repair dripping faucets and slow leaks promptly.
  • Watch for water stains, musty odors, or swollen trim.
  • Ask a qualified plumber to investigate unusual pressure or pipe noises.

Important safety reminder

Never enter standing water near electrical equipment. If water may be contaminated, a ceiling is sagging, or the area is unsafe, keep people and pets away and contact the appropriate emergency professional.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Burst pipe water damage FAQs

What should I do first when a pipe bursts?

Turn off the water supply if you can do so safely, avoid electrical hazards, protect belongings, and contact a water damage professional.

Can water damage exist behind walls?

Yes. Water can migrate behind drywall, under flooring, and into cabinets. Visible damage may not show the full extent of the moisture.

How soon should burst pipe water damage be addressed?

As soon as it is safe. Prompt assessment and drying can help reduce the chance of additional property damage.

Do I need to document the damage?

Photographs, videos, dates, and notes about the source of the leak can be useful when communicating with your insurance company.
